Build and deploy a working Hebrew-first platform that lets a student complete a real worksheet—from a photographed page to teacher feedback and parent acknowledgement—within one accessible learning workflow.
The pilot will test whether Machberet+ reduces app switching and adult assistance while making worksheet preparation and review practical for teachers.

The complete workflow

Capture → Confirm exercises → Assign → Solve → Review → Share → Acknowledge

A teacher or parent photographs or uploads a worksheet. Machberet+ extracts candidate exercises, an adult checks them, and the teacher assigns the approved tasks. The student completes one exercise at a time using their accessibility settings. The teacher reviews the answers and attaches voice feedback. The parent opens a secure link shared through WhatsApp and confirms that they reviewed the work together.

Five connected screens

| Screen | Required pilot functionality |
|---|---|
| 1. Smart Camera Scan & Auto-OCR Tasks | Capture a photo or upload an image/PDF page; crop, rotate, and correct perspective; recognize Hebrew text and supported math; preview detected exercises alongside the source; edit, select, and confirm exercises before assignment. Include manual exercise creation when recognition fails. |
| 2. Student Daily Homework Tray | Display one mission card at a time, with teacher audio instructions, a prominent start/resume action, and simple navigation. Separate completed work from pending work. |
| 3. Student Active Workbook | Present one active question with its answer area directly underneath. Support Hebrew text, isolated LTR math, vertical arithmetic with carry/borrow cells, simple fractions, and basic equations. Include read-aloud, spotlight focus, autosave, submission, and a collapsible AAC dock. |
| 4. Teacher Feedback & Talking Stickers | Show submitted answers beside their questions; mark reviewed work or request another attempt; attach a sticker with up to 15 seconds of recorded feedback; return work to the student or share the reviewed assignment with a parent. |
| 5. Parent Mobile Review | Open an assignment-specific link without creating an account; view the completed work, listen to feedback, and tap “Reviewed Together / עברנו יחד על הדף.” Save the acknowledgement for the teacher to see. |

Accessibility requirements

The pilot will include:

- Native Hebrew RTL layout with isolated LTR mathematical expressions.
- A calm palette, one visually dominant primary action per view, and an 85% spotlight mask around inactive worksheet content.
- Standard tablet targets of at least 64 × 64 CSS pixels, and gaze/motor-mode targets of at least 88 × 88, with 20px gaps and 48px edge clearance for primary controls.
- Configurable 800–1500ms dwell activation, visible progress, cancellation, and a pause function. App dwell and device-provided dwell must not cause duplicate activation.
- Keyboard navigation and two-switch scanning, including answer entry and correction.
- Hebrew read-aloud that preserves Nikud and speaks supported mathematical expressions meaningfully. Teachers can correct the spoken text or provide recorded instructions.
- Persistent student accessibility preferences and AAC messages such as help, clarification, break, and finished.

The student interface will limit competing actions through progressive disclosure. Keyboards and scanning groups need explicit treatment rather than an impractical blanket limit of seven keys.

Eye-gaze and switch support must be tested on the actual devices used by pilot participants; visual mockups alone do not establish compatibility.

Supporting components

This must be a working service with:

- Teacher accounts, protected student sessions, and teacher–student assignment permissions.
- Persistent storage for source pages, corrected exercises, answers, accessibility settings, audio feedback, and acknowledgements.
- A clear assignment lifecycle: draft → assigned → in progress → submitted → reviewed or returned → parent acknowledged.
- Recoverable saving, visible upload/recognition failures, and retry paths that preserve existing work.
- Expiring, revocable parent links restricted to one reviewed assignment.
- Teacher-initiated WhatsApp sharing through a share action or copied link.
- A downloadable, printable record of completed answers and feedback.
- Consent, deletion controls, and minimal measurement data for the pilot. No background camera, microphone, or gaze recording.

Initial content boundary

Start with clear printed Hebrew worksheets containing short instructions, short written answers, whole-number arithmetic, simple fractions, and basic one-variable equations. Process one worksheet page at a time.

OCR proposes content; an adult confirms its accuracy. Teacher review remains the authority for correctness.

Proposed field pilot

Run four weeks of classroom/home use after the working build passes readiness checks, with:

- 10–15 students, selected for varied access needs.
- 3–5 teachers or therapists and participating parents.
- At least three short assignments per student per week.
- A baseline observation using each student’s existing tools, followed by comparable Machberet+ assignments.

Measure interface assistance separately from teaching assistance. Compare each student with their own baseline and report results by input mode.

Proposed success criteria

| Outcome | Target |
|---|---|
| Complete workflow | At least 80% of assigned pilot worksheets reach teacher review without a technical blocker. |
| Student independence | At least 30% reduction in median adult interventions needed to operate the interface, compared with baseline. |
| Reduced fragmentation | At least 90% of student sessions require no additional worksheet/editor app; established assistive input tools remain available. |
| Scan preparation | Median capture-to-approved-assignment time of no more than three minutes for supported pages, including OCR corrections. |
| Teacher review | Median marking interaction of no more than ten seconds per short exercise, measured separately from reading and recording feedback. |
| Parent participation | At least 70% of shared assignments receive acknowledgement within 48 hours. |
| Reliability and access | No unresolved loss of acknowledged saved work, cross-student access defects, or blocking navigation failures in the tested configurations. |

Deferred beyond this pilot

Full Word/Google Docs import and export, editable Word OMML equations, formally validated accessible PDF export, handwriting recognition, advanced geometry, animated math, automatic grading, adaptive typing training, exam-accommodation workflows, distraction detection, automated WhatsApp messaging, and broad hardware certification.

These remain part of the product vision. The pilot first establishes whether the core worksheet workflow is useful, reliable, and accessible.

Definition of done: A participating student can receive a real scanned assignment, complete it using their configured access method, resume saved work, receive teacher voice feedback, and have a parent acknowledge it—with the entire sequence persisted and measurable.
